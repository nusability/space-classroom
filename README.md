# 🌞 Space Classroom — Sun, Earth & Moon

A little 3D simulator for explaining **day & night**, **the seasons**, and **the
phases of the Moon** to a child. Sizes and distances are *not* to scale — they're
chosen so things are easy to see and understand.

## How to open it

Just **double-click `index.html`** — it opens in any modern web browser
(Chrome, Safari, Firefox, Edge).

> The first time you open it, the page needs an internet connection to fetch the
> 3D graphics library. After that a connection still helps, since the library
> isn't stored offline.

## Controls

| Action | How |
|---|---|
| Circle around the view | Drag with the mouse / one finger |
| Zoom in & out | Scroll wheel / pinch |
| Switch what stays centred | **Look at: Earth / Moon / Sun** — or just **click the body itself** |
| See the Moon as we see it | **Moon from Earth** button |
| See the Earth as the Sun sees it | **Earth from Sun** button |
| Play / pause time | **Pause** button or the **spacebar** |
| Change how fast time runs | **Speed** slider, or 🐢 / 🚶 / 🚀 presets |
| Switch language | **EN / DE** (English or German) |
| Start over | **Reset** |

Whichever body you "look at" (Earth, Moon or Sun) always stays in the centre of
the screen — you orbit around *it*, so you can never lose it. The camera glides
smoothly when you switch. Both side panels can be collapsed by clicking their
headers.

Keyboard shortcuts: `Space` play/pause · `E` Earth · `M` Moon · `1` `2` `3`
lessons · `H` help.

## The three guided lessons

Each lesson button sets the camera, speed and helpers to show one idea clearly.

### 🕐 Day & Night
The Earth spins once a day. The half facing the Sun has daytime; the other half
is in shadow — night. Watch the line between light and dark sweep across the
surface. **Ask:** *"If it's daytime for us, what is it on the other side of the
Earth?"*

### 🍂 The Seasons
The Earth's axis (the red line) is **tilted**, and it always tilts the *same way*
in space — it does **not** swing around to face the Sun. So as the Earth circles
the Sun, each half leans toward the Sun for part of the year (summer) and away
for the rest (winter). Watch the "Sun overhead" read-out climb north and south.
**Ask:** *"Which pole is leaning toward the Sun right now? What season is that
half having?"*

### 🌙 Moon Phases
This lesson uses the **Moon from Earth** camera: you look out past the Earth
(kept in the foreground) at the Moon, the way we actually see it from home.
The Sun always lights exactly **one half** of the Moon. As the Moon circles the
Earth, we look at that lit half from changing angles — sometimes we see all of
it (Full Moon), sometimes none (New Moon), sometimes a sliver (Crescent). The
little Moon icon in the top-right corner shows the current phase.
**Ask:** *"The Sun is lighting up half the Moon — so why does it look like a
banana to us?"*

## The read-out panel (top right)

- **Date & season** for the Northern and Southern halves of the Earth.
- **Sun overhead** — the latitude where the Sun is directly above at noon. It
  swings between 23° North and 23° South over the year. That swing *is* the
  seasons.
- **Moon phase** with a live picture and how much of it is lit.

## Helpful toggles

- **Orbit paths** — the circular tracks, plus four labelled markers for the
  solstices and equinoxes.
- **Earth's axis** — the tilted red spin-axis, with a faint dashed "straight up"
  line next to it so the tilt is obvious. It grows a little when the camera is
  far away, so the tilt stays easy to see even from across the solar system.
- **Globe lines** — the Equator (gold), the two Tropics, and the polar circles.
- **Sunlight rays** — yellow arrows showing the light all arrives from one
  direction and only hits one side.

## A couple of honest notes for grown-ups

- The Moon here orbits in the same flat plane as the Earth. In real life its
  orbit is tilted a few degrees — which is why we *don't* get an eclipse every
  single month.
- The Moon keeps the same face toward Earth (that's real — it's called tidal
  locking).
- The time *ratios* are real: ~365 days in a year, ~29.5 days between same Moon
  phases. Only the sizes and gaps were shrunk to fit on screen.
